Darwinian evolution posits that all species arise and develop through the natural selection of small, inherited variations that increase an organism's ability to compete, survive, and reproduce. Within any population, individuals naturally exhibit slight differences in physical and behavioral traits. Because environmental resources are limited, a continuous struggle for existence occurs, meaning only a fraction of offspring survive to maturity. Individuals possessing traits advantageous to their specific environment are more likely to survive and pass those heritable traits to their offspring—a mechanism known as natural selection. Over successive generations, these cumulative adaptations alter the population's characteristics, driving descent with modification and giving rise to the vast diversity of life from common ancestors over deep time.
